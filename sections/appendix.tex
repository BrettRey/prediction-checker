\section{Estimation details}
\label{app:methods}

\subsection*{The outcome measure}
Take a revenue series with report baseline \(B_t\), forecast impact \(I_t\) and observed value \(Y_t\) for \(t=2016,\dots,2019\). Let \(y_t=\log B_t-\log Y_t\) (the observed shortfall below the baseline, positive when the outcome is below it) and \(\delta_t=\log B_t-\log(B_t-I_t)\) (the forecast shortfall). The model is
\[
y_t = k\,\delta_t + e_t,\qquad e_t=\sum_{s=2015}^{t}\varepsilon_s,\quad \varepsilon_s\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\ \text{independent},
\]
so the baseline's error is a random walk from 2014, the last actual year in the report, with \(\operatorname{Cov}(e_t,e_u)=\sigma^2\min(t-2014,\,u-2014)\). With \(\Sigma\) that covariance matrix, \(\hat k=\delta'\Sigma^{-1}y/\delta'\Sigma^{-1}\delta\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat k)=1/\delta'\Sigma^{-1}\delta\). The error scale \(\sigma\) is set by calibration rather than estimated from four observations, so the 95\% interval is \(\hat k\pm1.96\) standard errors with no degrees-of-freedom correction; it covers uncertainty about how far the baseline would have drifted, not uncertainty in measuring the outcomes, which are administrative totals. The band of \(k\) implied by the forecasters' cited inputs projects the low- and high-input scenario paths onto \(\delta\) in the same metric. A model-implied scenario's \(k\) is the same projection of the report's chain recomputed at a given uptake path, holding the report's other inputs fixed.

\subsection*{Design analysis}
Before any outcome data was opened, each quantity was simulated 20,000 times (seed 20261003) in two worlds: without the forecast effect, \(Y_t=B_t e^{e_t}\), and with it, \(Y_t=B_te^{e_t}-I_t\). Each simulated path was assigned to whichever of the report's two paths gives it the higher Gaussian likelihood under \(\Sigma\). \enquote{Classified correctly} is the share of paths assigned to the world that generated them; the figures in the text are the smaller of the two worlds' shares, at the volatility of the report's own 2010--2014 data.

\subsection*{Calibrating the baseline's error}
The report checked its forecast of Canadian streaming subscribers against its forecast for the United States \citep[para.~137, p.~51]{nordicity2015television}, so the error in that US forecast is evidence about the technology side of its baseline that Canadian regulation couldn't affect. The pre-stated rule sets \(\sigma\) to the larger of the series' 2010--2014 volatility and \(|\log(\text{observed}/\text{forecast})|/\sqrt{h}\) for US Netflix subscribers in 2018 (\(h=4\) years from 2014). The other calibrations apply the same formula to 2016 and 2017, pool 2016--2018 as a random walk, or measure the error in growth from 2015, where the report's starting figure already differed from Netflix's count.
