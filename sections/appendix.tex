\section{Estimation details}
\label{app:methods}

\subsection*{The outcome measure}
Take a revenue series with report baseline \(B_t\), forecast impact \(I_t\) and observed value \(Y_t\) for \(t=2016,\dots,2019\). Let \(y_t=\log B_t-\log Y_t\) (the observed shortfall below the baseline, positive when the outcome is below it) and \(\delta_t=\log B_t-\log(B_t-I_t)\) (the forecast shortfall). The model is
\[
y_t = k\,\delta_t + e_t,\qquad e_t=\sum_{s=2015}^{t}\varepsilon_s,\quad \varepsilon_s\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\ \text{independent},
\]
so the outcome without the decisions would have been \(B_te^{-e_t}\), and the baseline's error \(e_t\) is positive when the baseline overstates it. The error is a random walk from 2014, the last actual year in the report, and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(e_t,e_u)=\sigma^2\min(t-2014,\,u-2014).
\]
With \(\Sigma\) that covariance matrix, \(\hat k=\delta'\Sigma^{-1}y/\delta'\Sigma^{-1}\delta\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat k)=1/\delta'\Sigma^{-1}\delta\). The error scale \(\sigma\) is set by calibration rather than estimated from four observations, so the 95\% interval is \(\hat k\pm1.96\) standard errors with no degrees-of-freedom correction; it covers uncertainty about how far the baseline would have drifted, not uncertainty in measuring the outcomes, which are administrative totals. The band of \(k\) implied by the forecasters' cited inputs projects the low- and high-input scenario paths onto \(\delta\) in the same metric. A model-implied scenario's \(k\) is the same projection of the report's chain recomputed at a given uptake path, holding the report's other inputs fixed. Table~\ref{tab:inputs} gives \(B_t\), \(I_t\) and its components, \(Y_t\), \(\delta_t\) and \(y_t\) for both series, and tables~\ref{tab:scenarios} and~\ref{tab:calibrations} the uptake paths and the values of \(\sigma\).

\subsection*{Design analysis}
Before any CRTC outcome file was opened, I simulated each quantity 20,000 times (seed 20261003) in two worlds: without the forecast effect, \(Y_t=B_te^{-e_t}\), so that \(y_t=e_t\) and \(k=0\); and with it, \(Y_t=B_te^{-e_t}-I_t\), the forecast impact applied in levels to the drifted baseline. The second gives \(y_t=\delta_t+e_t\) (\(k=1\)) up to a term of order \(e_tI_t/(B_t-I_t)\). The simulation code writes the drift with the opposite sign, \(B_te^{e_t}\); since the errors are symmetric about zero, the two describe the same distribution. The extra term matters for coverage, because it scales the error in \(y_t\) by about \(B_t/(B_t-I_t)\): applied to paths simulated this way, the 95\% interval covers \(k=1\) in \lttvCoverDollarsSpec{} of them for channels and \lttvCoverDollarsBDU{} for distributors, against 95\% when the impact applies proportionally, \(Y_t=(B_t-I_t)e^{-e_t}\) (\texttt{scripts/model\_check\_lttv.py}, seed 20261003). The estimation model is the proportional one, and the intervals in the text are valid under it, given \(\sigma\). Each simulated path was assigned to whichever of the report's two paths gives it the higher Gaussian likelihood under \(\Sigma\). \enquote{Classified correctly} is the share of paths assigned to the world that generated them; the figures in the text are the smaller of the two worlds' shares, at the volatility of the report's own 2010--2014 data.

\subsection*{Calibrating the baseline's error}
The report checked its forecast of Canadian streaming penetration against a US forecast built from Trefis's projection of US Netflix subscribers and Digital TV Research's of all subscription streaming \citep[paras.~135--137, pp.~50--51]{nordicity2015television}. The error in the Netflix part of that US forecast is therefore evidence about the technology side of the report's baseline, assumed unaffected by Canadian regulation; the calibration uses the Netflix row of Table~7, whose outcomes Netflix itself reports. The pre-stated rule sets \(\sigma\) to the larger of the series' 2010--2014 volatility and \(|\log(\text{observed}/\text{forecast})|/\sqrt{h}\) for US Netflix subscribers in 2018 (\(h=4\) years from 2014). The other calibrations apply the same formula to 2016 and 2017, pool 2016--2018 as a random walk, or measure the error in growth from 2015, where the report's starting figure already differed from Netflix's count. With \(e_t=\log(\text{observed}_t/\text{forecast}_t)\), the pooled value is
\[
\sqrt{\bigl(e_{2016}^2/2+(e_{2017}-e_{2016})^2+(e_{2018}-e_{2017})^2\bigr)/3},
\]
the maximum-likelihood s.d.\ of a random walk from 2014 observed at \(h=2,3,4\); the growth-based values replace \(e_t\) with \(e_t-e_{2015}\) and count \(h\) from 2015.
