## Risk Test

- Strongest desk-rejection risk: "not about economics as a profession". Resolution in the manuscript: page one frames the paper as a check of an economic forecast used in policy, in the line of EJW's forecast checks and Kane (2026), and says why consultants' impact studies matter to economists (multiplier-based job and GDP claims in policy debate).
- Strongest reviewer-rejection risk: the counterfactual. The specialty/pay result depends on how wrong the report's no-reform baseline was; the multiverse shows the verdict turns inconclusive under three of nine calibrations. Resolution: lead with the stable point estimate and state the fragility plainly; the pass-through comparison (a fall of about $358M implied against a rise of $111M) doesn't need the baseline.
- Strongest "not motivated" risk: "one consultancy report; so what?" Resolution: the forecast was put to a parliamentary committee as the key evidence against a national regulatory decision; the paper adds a stakeholder impact forecast to a literature that mostly studies regulators' cost estimates.
- Strongest "opinion piece" risk: low; the paper is numbers from public files with code.
- Strongest "wrong literature" risk: missing the media-economics literature on bundling (Crawford and Yurukoglu 2012) and the input-output critique literature (Crompton 2006; Siegfried and Zimbalist 2000). Both need reading before submission (library access).
