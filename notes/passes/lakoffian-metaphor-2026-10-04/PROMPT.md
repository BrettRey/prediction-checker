## The pass (lakoffian-metaphor, from the portfolio's pass registry)

Four questions, in order. The checklist requires all four
(Project-Management/templates/paper-submission-checklist.md:82).

1. WHICH SOURCE DOMAINS ORGANIZE THE PROSE? Read the manuscript for the
   governing frames, not for individual figures of speech. The recurring ones
   in this portfolio: war and combat, court and jurisdiction, engineering and
   load-bearing structure, topology and space, field and practice, ecology,
   market, machinery, and containment. List each with the passages that carry
   it. A frame that appears once is a figure of speech; a frame that organizes
   a section is doing argumentative work.

2. WHAT DO THOSE DOMAINS ENTAIL? For each frame, write out what it brings with
   it. CATEGORIES ARE CONTAINERS entails sharp boundaries and an inside and
   outside, which is exactly what a projectibility-first account denies.
   ARGUMENT IS WAR entails a winner. THEORY IS A BUILDING entails foundations
   that fail catastrophically rather than degrade.

3. DO THE ENTAILMENTS FIT THE ARGUMENT, OR STEER IT? This is the pass's real
   work. Flag any place where the frame's entailment does a job the evidence
   has not done: where a contested inference reads as settled because the
   metaphor already granted it. Boundary and container talk in a paper arguing
   for graded profiles is the standard case here.

4. ARE MIXED FRAMES DELIBERATE? Two source domains inside one passage are fine
   when staged. Flag the accidental ones, where a sentence starts in mechanics
   and ends in ecology.

Check figure and diagram vocabulary against the same frames. A diagram is a
metaphor with a layout, and a box-and-arrow figure imports containment and
causal-flow entailments whether or not the prose does.
