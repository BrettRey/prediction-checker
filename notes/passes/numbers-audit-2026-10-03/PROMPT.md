## What to do, for every numeric claim in main.txt

Include counts, percentages, dollar amounts, years used as data, intervals, standard errors, multiples ("5.0 standard errors"), page/paragraph locators attached to numbers, and numbers inside quotations.

1. FIND THE SOURCE. The CSV row, script computation, or source-document passage (with paragraph, table or page). If there is none, that is the finding.
2. COMPARE EXACTLY. Not "approximately right". Check rounding is consistent with the source value and that a percentage and its underlying count agree.
3. CHECK DERIVED NUMBERS. Totals that should equal the sum of their parts; percentages that should fall inside stated intervals; distances in standard errors that should equal |a − b| / SE; the same quantity stated in two places (abstract vs body, text vs table vs figure caption). Cross-section disagreement is the commonest drift.
4. CHECK THE PIPELINE ON A SAMPLE. For at least five numbers chosen from different scripts, recompute from the raw or derived data yourself (e.g., k at the pre-stated sigma by GLS from table A2's inputs; a CPI-deflated change; a share in table A1; a Netflix calibration value; an uptake share from the CRTC count and subscriber total). Say which you recomputed and how.
5. FLAG RED-FLAG SHAPES: round numbers, plausible-sounding ranges, statistics with no traceable file.
6. Also flag any sentence whose wording misdescribes its number (wrong direction, wrong base for a percentage, "most" where the share is a minority, a forecast year called actual, and so on).
