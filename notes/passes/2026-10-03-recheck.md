**4. What a referee should additionally see.**

- **The split is an accounting identity with an imposed scenario allocation.** Subscriber counts provide an additional observable, but revenue per subscriber is calculated from the same revenue and subscriber totals. It is not an independent observation of prices or a separate causal validation.

- **The base changes the meaning of “loss.”** In the observed 2014–2019 nominal revenue decline, falling subscribers are partly offset by rising revenue per subscriber. In the shortfall relative to the growing baseline, missing per-subscriber growth accounts for roughly **68% of the log shortfall**. Both statements are true; they answer different questions.

- **The unaligned 2019 gaps are different:** subscribers are **5.8737%** below the report’s baseline and revenue per subscriber **7.4071%** below. The manuscript’s **3.9%** and **8.1%** are changes in relative gaps after alignment to 2014, not those raw level differences.

- **The counted-uptake comparison holds more than uptake fixed.** It retains the original exemption-order and closure dollar losses. Updating only the ¶220 blended-ARPU interaction would add approximately **$6.42m** to the 2019 exemption loss and change the per-subscriber shortfall from **2.483% to 2.551%**. This is small substantively, but demonstrates why the exercise should be described as component scaling rather than an exact rerun.

- **Normalization cannot guarantee continuing comparability.** Non-reporting BDUs, missing returns, revisions, and calendar/broadcast-year conventions deserve one explicit limitation. Moreover, the numerator is total BDU revenue, including equipment and other revenue; the resulting ratio is broader than a package price.

These qualifications preserve the useful descriptive finding: after alignment, both subscriber numbers and revenue per subscriber fall farther below the report’s baseline than in the specified fixed low-uptake scenario. They prevent that finding from being read as identification of the mechanism responsible.
