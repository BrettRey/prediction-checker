## The pass (rhetoric-and-humour, from the portfolio's pass registry)

Two halves. Do them in one read but report them separately.

RHETORICAL FIGURES. Look for places where the argument has a shape the prose
could carry: parallelism, antithesis, chiasmus, isocolon, staged recurrence,
contrastive sequence. Two directions of work:

- Where a figure is present, ask whether it earns its place. A figure is
  earned when the argument genuinely has that shape, so the form helps the
  reader hold it. It is ornamental padding when the symmetry is imposed on
  asymmetric content, and it is a substitute for an argument when the
  parallelism is the only reason a contrast seems to hold.
- Where the argument has a shape and the prose does not, say so. A genuine
  three-way contrast written as three unrelated sentences is harder to read
  than it needs to be. Do not manufacture these; propose only where the
  structure is already in the content.

HUMOUR. Brett's papers carry wit deliberately. Check each instance against
three questions: does it help the point, does it fit the venue, does it
undercut the register. A joke that lands but makes a contested claim feel
obvious is a metaphor problem as much as a humour problem, so flag it for
lakoffian-metaphor too. Note that venue is part of the judgment, so this pass
goes stale on a retarget even when the prose has not moved.

Report: figures found and whether earned; places where the argument's shape is
available and unused; humour instances with a verdict on each. Then apply what
Brett accepts.
